Prehistoric synth for the M-VAVE FM-1

Free firmware that turns the FM-1 into a six-track groovebox from the age of giants: creature calls, thunder and lava, drones and war drums, a live sequencer and a song mode. Try it in your browser, then put it on your FM-1.

What is PALEOTONE?

The M-VAVE FM-1 is a small, inexpensive synth with 27 keys, eight knobs and a colour screen. PALEOTONE is new firmware for it: you install it over USB from a web page, and the FM-1 becomes a different instrument.

It's a groovebox you play live. Six tracks run at once: three synths, a track of prehistoric sound effects, a track of creature calls and a drum machine. You record by playing, loop by loop, stack passes on top of each other, punch in effects with one finger, and arrange sections into a song, all from the FM-1's own panel.

The look is a 1993 park-operations terminal: phosphor green on black, amber for what needs attention, and one chunky typeface, Clayborn, drawn large on every screen so it reads from across the room.

PALEOTONE is free and open source (GPL-3.0), built on SLOOP by isod89 and Felucca by Leo Kuroshita. Everything SLOOP does is still here.

Creatures on the keys

SAURUS is a voice built for animals: a throat, a mouth and a breath, shaped by SIZE, SWOOP, GROWL and WILD. Each note is one call. CALL KIT puts every creature on the keys at once.

TYRANT ROARRAPTOR BARKPTERO SHRIEKHADRO HORNBRACHIO CALLBABY REXSEA MONSTERCOMPY PACKSWAMP CROAKNEST CHIRPS

ROAR plays drones and drum ensembles, with its RING page to let the drones ring through the rests.

And SFX puts the world around them on the keys: thunder, rain, a volcano, lava, footsteps, wings, a river, a tar pit.

Early alpha. PALEOTONE is experimental firmware. An install can leave the FM-1 unable to start and in need of recovery. Back up your projects with the editor first, and read the recovery notes before you install. M-VAVE's own updater takes the FM-1 back to the official firmware.
