# Dinosynth research for T-WRECKS

Research checkpoint: 10 November 2026, reviewing candidate 464e9c7. The audit below describes that earlier candidate. Subsequent user guidance asks for the weight and drive of a march, without requiring a literal march or elaborate march-specific structure. The [completion plan](TWRECKS-NEXT-PLAN.md) records the current implementation and remaining listening work.

## Finding

The current candidate covers the atmospheric end of the intended instrument. Artist statements and release documentation support a wider dinosynth vocabulary: substantial keyboard orchestration, recurring musical ideas, deliberate rhythmic weight, dramatic scenes and, in some branches, metal or synthwave influences. The heavy bass-and-marching-drums scene Dylan requested is a valid direction within that range. It requires a different rhythmic/compositional profile from the existing ambient scheduler.

No single tempo, scale, drum pattern, instrument or production quality defines every dinosynth release. These sources establish a range of practices, not a formula that all artists follow. Our choice is to emphasize recognizable musical scenes and physical weight while retaining useful quiet scenes.

## Method and limits

The review prioritizes artist interviews, artist release notes and label descriptions. It covers five central projects—Diplodocus, Thagomizer, Synthosaurus, Primal Beast and Archosaur—plus an eight-artist anthology and Paleowolf as an adjacent prehistoric/ritual reference. The source register contains an interview and eighteen release references, with paired artist/label documentation where useful.

This is documentary and production research. I have not performed a complete critical listening pass or transcribed the recordings. No BPM estimates, harmonic transcriptions, frequency measurements or claims of personally hearing particular timbres are presented as measured findings. Proposed motifs, registers and tempos below are engineering/composition proposals, clearly distinguished from artist statements. Supporter reviews on album pages are not treated as statements by the musician. Search results for the unrelated Tesla Coils song named Dinosynth were excluded from the genre evidence.

The next musical acceptance step must include direct reference listening and comparison with our own arrangements. Passing level, stability and repeat-render tests cannot establish stylistic success.

## Working musical brief — design proposals, not universal genre rules

### Weight

Weight should come from a defined low-register part, clear attacks, deliberate accents and space around those attacks. For the aggressive scene, use a deep mono synth bass with sufficient upper harmonics to remain audible on the FM-1 speaker. A low-frequency fundamental alone is not a reliable test of perceived weight. Start with existing bass voices before adding another DSP layer.

The heavy scene needs a coherent, weighty pulse, with repeated footfalls and intentional reinforcement. The present random sparse-hit approach remains useful for ambience; it cannot be the only rhythm architecture. Variation should operate on a retained pattern or phrase—accent changes, omissions, a short fill, a turnaround—so the underlying pulse remains recognizable.

### Melodic and harmonic identity

Provide a motif or bass figure that returns, with a clear answer or cadence. Develop it over several bars while preserving something the listener can recognize. Slow harmony can support a strong rhythmic identity. Choose modal or chromatic material for the scene and available voices; do not treat one scale as a genre requirement.

Brass-like, organ, choir, string and other deliberately synthetic timbres are candidate orchestration colors. A source mentioning orchestral brass is not a requirement to imitate a natural orchestra. The FM-1 can sound synthetic and still communicate scale, menace and grandeur.

### Form and production

Use purposeful sections: introduction, established figure, answer, reduction and return. Give an aggressive scene its own transition into a full, driving arrangement. Keep low bass and drum attacks readable through the effects. Evaluate saturation, filtering and restricted bandwidth by listening, rather than adding blanket hiss as a shortcut to age or grit.

Creature calls should be expressive events the player can place. Manual control is a separate usability requirement from whether a source recording uses effects. The music should retain its identity when the calls are turned off.

## Audit of the installed candidate

These findings are from the current source, not a subjective audition:

| Current behavior | Why it constrains the requested direction | Planned response |
|---|---|---|
| All twenty factory tempos are 60–84 BPM. | The bank occupies a narrow tempo region; tempo alone is not the cause of the ambient bias. | Use a distinct rhythmic and phrasing profile for the heavy scene; explore a moderate marching pulse by ear. |
| Factory recipes share many attack, release, motion, repeat and effect settings. | Changing sound lists does not create twenty different arrangement designs. | Revisit scene structure, articulation, motifs and rhythm in representative prototypes first. |
| Percussion considers only a downbeat strike, step 10, and step 14 on selected bars. | At most three candidate hits cannot sustain the requested regular marching part. | Add a patterned march profile with repeatable accents, while retaining the sparse profile for suitable scenes. |
| Bass is assigned through Sequence 2; its level is zero in most recipes. | Deep bass has no persistent leading role in the present bank. | Make Sequence 2 an explicit bass role in the aggressive arrangement and coordinate its rhythm with drums. |
| Calls are automatic bar-line events with a three-second gate and cooldown. | Changing Chance does not let the player place a call precisely. | Add manual triggering, optional automatic mode and manual priority. |
| Home ALGORITHM currently handles Calls; other musical voices are selected from their layer pages. | Voice choice is not obvious or consistent in the central performance flow. | Choose the track on Home, use ALGORITHM for its voice/character, and show the chosen name. |

Installed candidate memory measurements: image 530,064 of 581,564 bytes; RAM 90,476 of 98,304; pool 333,448 of 344,064. The remaining space suggests small control and pattern changes are practical candidates. It does not establish CPU headroom. Direct CPU readings remain required with the bass, drums, effects and a manually triggered call active together.

## Focused close-listening queue

1. Diplodocus — Slow And Heavy, plus Return of the Thunder Lizard and Primal Rage from the B-side: establish the core reference palette and scene development.
2. Thagomizer — March Of The Sauropods and Migration Of The Camarasaurus: assess how a sense of movement and scale is sustained.
3. Synthosaurus — Metalosauric Crush and Prehistoric Gods: assess the aggressive branch, separating arrangement weight from recording texture.
4. Primal Beast — Pterodactyl the Brave: follow the documented FM/bass/drum resource combination and longer development.
5. Archosaur — Ancient Times and a spread of the anthology: check melodic/ambient breadth and avoid narrowing the device to one aesthetic.
6. Thagomizer — Dinosaur Drone II and Paleowolf — Prehistoric Meditations: retain a clear comparison for the deliberate ambient end.

For each reference, record instrumentation as actually heard, recurring ideas, bass role, drum placement, form, use of silence, call placement and production character. Only measured or transcribed timings should be described as such. Then compare our prototypes under similar listening conditions, with calls muted for one pass.
